# Humanize Writing

AI text gets spotted for two reasons: it is *too even* (same sentence length, same rhythm, same tidy structure) and it is *too vague* (big words, no specifics, no opinion). Human writing is uneven and specific, and someone is clearly behind it. The job is not to scramble words or add typos. It is to put a real person back into the text.

## Workflow

1. **Identify the target.** Language (Arabic dialect or MSA, English), channel (email, lecture, social post, message), and audience (fellows, colleagues, patients, public). Ask one short question only if the channel is truly unclear; otherwise infer and say what you assumed.
2. **Keep the facts untouched.** Never change a number, drug, dose, cut-off, guideline, or citation while rewriting. If a medical claim looks wrong or unsupported, flag it in a note instead of silently "fixing" or smoothing it.
3. **Diagnose** the draft against the tells below (silently, no need to list them all).
4. **Rewrite** by applying the moves below.
5. **Return** the rewritten text first, then at most 2-3 lines on what changed. If the draft lacks anything only the user knows (a real case, an opinion, a number from their own practice), add a clearly marked placeholder like `[مثال من حالة عندك]` / `[your own case here]` rather than inventing one. Invented anecdotes are fabrication, not humanizing.

## The tells (what to remove)

- **Opening throat-clearing:** "In today's fast-paced world", "في عالمنا المتسارع اليوم", "من الجدير بالذكر", "لا شك أن".
- **Closing summaries that repeat the text:** "In conclusion", "وفي الختام يمكن القول".
- **Rule-of-three lists** and the "not only X, but also Y" / "ليس فقط... بل أيضاً" frame, used everywhere.
- **Em-dash and colon overuse**, "It's not X, it's Y" contrasts, rhetorical question then immediate answer.
- **Stock vocabulary:** delve, landscape, tapestry, pivotal, crucial, leverage, robust, seamless, holistic, unlock, navigate; Arabic equivalents like "يلعب دوراً محورياً", "نسيج", "رحلة", "في غاية الأهمية", "لا يخفى على أحد".
- **Uniform paragraphs:** every paragraph the same length, every bullet the same shape, bold headings on a 3-line message.
- **Hedging everything** ("may potentially help") or **praising everything** ("great question!").
- **Emoji and hashtag confetti** in professional text.
- **Zero specifics:** no names, numbers, places, dates, or cases.

## The moves (what to add)

- **Vary rhythm hard.** Mix a 4-word sentence with a 30-word one. A fragment is fine. Start some sentences with "And", "But", "و", "بس", "لكن".
- **Be specific.** Replace "studies show benefit" with the actual trial, number, or the thing you saw on the screen last Tuesday. Specifics are the strongest human signal.
- **Take a position.** Say what you would do, what trips fellows up, what you disagree with in the guideline. "I never trust a single-view EF" beats "EF assessment has limitations".
- **Use first person and direct address** where the channel allows: "I", "you", "أنا بشوف", "تخيل إنك قدام مريض...".
- **Prefer plain words.** "Use" not "utilize". "نستخدم" not "نستعين بـ" unless the register calls for it.
- **Let it be imperfect.** Slightly informal connectors, a parenthetical aside, an honest "I'm not 100% sure about this one". Do not add fake typos.
- **Match length to the channel.** A WhatsApp reply is two lines. Cut anything that exists only to sound complete.
- **Structure only where a human would.** Lecture handouts can have headings and bullets; an email to a colleague should not.

## Channel notes

- **Teaching fellows:** sound like a consultant at the echo machine. Use "look at this", a clinical pearl, a common mistake, a question that makes them commit to an answer before you reveal it. Keep English medical terms in English inside Arabic text (LVEF, GLS, E/e', MR vena contracta).
- **Emails:** short, direct, one ask, a normal sign-off. No "I hope this email finds you well" unless the user would genuinely write it.
- **Social posts:** one idea, a human hook, no hashtag wall (0-3 relevant ones).
- **Arabic:** write in the dialect the user asks for; default to light Egyptian/Gulf-neutral conversational Arabic for social and teaching, MSA for formal letters and abstracts. Read `references/arabic-and-english-patterns.md` for before/after examples and AI-sounding Arabic phrases to avoid.

## Honest limits

No rewrite can guarantee that an AI-detector will pass the text, and chasing detector scores produces worse writing. Aim for text the user would be comfortable signing. If the text will be submitted somewhere with an AI-disclosure rule (journal, exam, institution), remind the user to follow it.
